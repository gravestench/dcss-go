Every broad category needs a follow-up feature/content inventory before a full-port completion claim. The fixture entries do not count as a port of their categories.

Review follow-up: the Human/Fighter starting-skill resolver checks that a weapon skill has a species aptitude but does not independently reject a non-weapon skill ID in malformed future weapon metadata. The committed starter catalog uses valid weapon skills. Add that membership check when extending the importer or supporting more starts.

Run the slice with `go run ./cmd/dcrawl -seed 1 -save dcrawl-save.json` from this repository. On startup, choose Dungeon Crawl or the saved-run entry. Human (`j`), Fighter (`a`), and weapons (`a`–`f`) are the playable choices; enter a name before or after those choices. During play, move with `h/j/k/l/y/u/b/n` or arrows, wait with `.` or `s`, pick up with `g` or `,`, take stairs with `<` or `>`, open inventory with `i`, skills with `m`, and help with `?`. `S` confirms save and exit, Ctrl-S saves and exits immediately, and Ctrl-Q confirms abandonment. `q` means quaff and `L` means run right; both are currently free unavailable actions. Menus, notices, and resizes do not spend auts.

The 79×24 canvas uses the pinned source's pane geometry and field positions. At larger sizes, the dungeon expands to fill the additional terminal area as requested. The playable map is still a fixed two-floor fixture, so its terrain, monster encounters, experience, defenses, inventory effects, training, and other unported gameplay cannot match a normal DCSS run. The HUD shows `--` for those missing values rather than inventing them. Menu text and order come from committed source-traced content; the Go game never reads the C++ checkout to launch. Source console captures are visual references, while the executable's compiled version differs from the pinned source revision used by the generated banner.
